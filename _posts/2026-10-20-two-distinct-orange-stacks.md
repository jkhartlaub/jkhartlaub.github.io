Imagine a bunch of equal-sized oranges on a table, all sides touching, where a slice of the top view is  
⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪  
with empty spaces ⚪ and where, in a healthy dose of imagination, we perceive the distances between the 🟠 as equilateral!  
  
Now put a second sheet of now 🔵 oranges in the dips and we get the top-view slice  
⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪🔵⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🔵⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪  
Repeating this alternating pattern with math-exercise-worthy amounts leads to what chemists call the 𝗵𝗲𝘅𝗮𝗴𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗹𝗼𝘀𝗲-𝗽𝗮𝗰𝗸𝗲𝗱 lattice (𝗵𝗰𝗽), which, remarkably, achieves the maximal possible sphere packing density of 74%!  
  
In contrast, putting the third layer of now 🟢 oranges in the dips  
⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪🟢⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟢⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟢⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪⚪⚪⚪🔵⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🔵⚪⚪⚪⚪  
⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪⚪⚪⚪⚪🟠⚪  
leads to the 𝗳𝗮𝗰𝗲-𝗰𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝗰𝘂𝗯𝗶𝗰 (𝗳𝗰𝗰) lattice which also achieves the maximum packing density.  
  
Experiments [1] show that the prototypical crystal solid argon prefers the fcc lattice at low, and the hcp lattice at high pressure, but 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗶𝘇𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝘂𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 has been challenging, in particular since the relevant quantities associated, the lattice sums, agree up to over 99.99%!  
  
A recent Phys. Rev. B paper by, among others, our New Zealand colleagues [****Andrés Robles Navarro****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andr%C3%A9s-robles-navarro-941441162/) & [****Peter Schwerdtfeger****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/peter-schwerdtfeger-8b90ba26/), discovered a distinct 𝗯𝗶𝗳𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗽𝗼𝗶𝗻𝘁 on a smooth transformation between these lattices [2]. In our new preprint with Norwegian colleagues [****Morten Hammer****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/morten-hammer-35521a5b/) [****Øivind Wilhelmsen****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/%C3%B8ivind-wilhelmsen-698b312b8/) and first author [****Tage Maltby****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/tage-maltby-178a65211/), we investigate this transition at finite pressure and under the inclusion of 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝘆-𝗯𝗼𝗱𝘆 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀 [3], where [****Andreas Alexander Buchheit****](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andreas-alexander-buchheit/) and I contribute a general method for the treatment of the multi-atomic many-body lattice sums in terms of generalized zeta functions, the 𝗮𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗼𝘁𝗿𝗼𝗽𝗶𝗰 𝗘𝗽𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗶𝗻 𝘇𝗲𝘁𝗮 𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀, introduced in our other new preprint [4] whose content has been the main project of my PhD.  
  
In particular, the density functional theory treatment by our colleagues suggests that due to many-body interactions the path between hcp and fcc bypasses the body-centered cubic lattice (bcc), which is good since another oranges-visualization would exceed the LinkedIn post character limit ⛔
